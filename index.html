Case Study 01 • 0-to-1 Launch

EV cars navigation platform, 0-to-1 Launch

Role & Scope Lead PM (Strategy, Architecture, Specs, Delivery)
Target Users Front End teams and Car Drivers
Team & Timeline 5 Engineers - Backend, Data Science, Algorithm, DevOps and Automation| 14 Months
Build navigation routes for EV cars
Target: Establish a team and build MVP product | Result: MVP product launch after 10 months

1. The Problem & Business Context

Charging EV cars is a big challenge where charging point availability, price, fit and location makes it hard for drivers to plan their trip

2. Discovery & Key Trade-Offs

  • User Insight: We identified that drivers hate to depart the highway to charge and are keen to know the waiting time on the station which currently they can't tell
  • Trade-Off Made: Live traffic updates and tier-level pricing

3. Execution & Delivery

We broke the MVP to smaller milestones which included: backend infrastructure, simple A* algorithm implementation, and naive battery usage, we then implemented a physical-based battery usage model, integrated average speed data and weather and the last milestone was to include charging station preferences into the algorithm, the MVP was delivered to beta drivers after 8 months with a complete DevOps infrastructure to backup failures and for fast deployment of new features

4. Impact & Key Learnings

We observed fast responses (p95 under 2 seconds), mostly accurate time and distance calculation and routes that needed improvement based on driver's preferences, our key learnings were to expand the testing suite with more routes in different departure times

Case Study 02 • Operational excellence

Game day operational efficiency

Role & Scope Lead PM (Strategy, Architecture, Specs, Delivery)
Target Users Box office users & game-day fans
Team & Timeline 4 Full-stack Engineers | 4 Months
Improve fans' game day experience
Target: Excel troubleshooting tools, shorten queues and enable smooth stadium ingress

1. The Problem & Business Context

Fans with problems are sent from the queue to the box office, which creates long queues in the box office and increases fan and staff frustration, game day experience has a heavy effect on client's and fans overall impression of the company

2. Discovery & Key Trade-Offs

  • User Insight: Talking to different box office cashiers, managers and observing scanning attempts on end users' app helped us focus on the important aspects of game day experience: fast service, better orientation and proactive troubleshooting
  • Trade-Off Made: We wanted to create a mobile/tablet-first with abilities to solve complex scenarios near the gates, but settled on helping field agents to triage between directing people to the right gates, help them find the game's relevant barcodes or go to the box office for advanced troubleshooting

3. Execution & Delivery

We first focused on breaking the delivery into an MVP->Feedback->improvements stages where the main focus was the ability to scan fans' barcodes with tablet or mobile devices, to proactively find orientation problems, invalid barcodes and help direct people with extra information to the box office, we delivered a first MVP version after 3 months and on-time for the pre-season games which tend to have lower volume and later delivered fixes and improvements into the season itself

4. Impact & Key Learnings

We reduced the box-office queues on game day by 20% and also shortened the service time by 25% (from 8 minutes to 6), one of the most important key learnings was that we need to invest more time in offline services and find alternatives to weak Wi-Fi standards in the stadium

Case Study 03 • Growth & Retention

Build retention and growth around ticket forwarding

Role & Scope Senior PM
Target Users Fans and Ticket purchasers online
Team & Timeline 3 Engineers, 1 Designer | 3 Months
Increase retention and fan base online
Target: Add self-service action to attract fans visiting the client's (Team/Theatre) site

1. Diagnosis

We identified that a large number of fans call the box office for actions like ticket forwarding that can be self-served online

2. Hypothesis & Experiment Design

  • Hypothesis: We assumed that adding a ticket forwarding option will increase the number of online visits and grow the fan base
  • Experimentation: We opened resell/forward options to 5000 fans online and tracked if they visited the site more and whether their actions led to new fans being added to the system

3. Results & Iteration

We saw that fan retention rate increased by 8% as part of the A/B testing, mostly for forwarding tickets, and fan base increased by 12% (every 8th fan that forwarded a ticket created a new fan, usually a friend or a family member)

Our follow up was asking the box office personnel to inform fans that the action is available online each time fans call
